15 Instagram content ideas for interior designers

The best Instagram content for an interior design firm comes from its own sites. Progress photos, material choices, problems solved and finished rooms. Below are 15 ideas, grouped by project stage. Each one uses photos you already take.

Before the work starts

1. The brief, in one line.

Post the empty room with the client's brief as the caption. "Make it feel bigger without moving a wall." Followers stay for the answer.

2. The mood board.

Show the references that shaped the design. Carousel format works: one board, then three close-ups.

3. The material shortlist.

Three tile samples on the floor of the real room. Ask which one you chose. Reveal it in a later post.

4. Measuring day.

A short Reel of the site survey. It shows care before a single purchase.

During the build

5. Site progress, same angle.

Shoot every visit from one fixed spot. Five weeks later, you have a time-lapse carousel.

6. The problem you solved.

A beam in the wrong place. A slab that was not level. Show it, then show the fix. This proves skill better than any finished photo.

7. Craft close-ups.

Joinery, edge details, grout lines. Short captions naming the trade.

8. Voice-note captions.

Record 30 seconds after a site visit. Turn it into the caption. It sounds like you, because it is you.

9. The decision explained.

Why this lighting, why that height. One photo, one reason.

10. Behind the delivery.

Furniture arriving, the install team at work. Real people build trust.

The finished room

11. Before and after, honestly.

Same angle, same lens height. Clean the site mess with editing if you like. Never change the design. The room in the post must be the room the client got.

12. The walkthrough Reel.

One slow pass through the room. Let the camera move; keep the edits few.

13. One room, five details.

A carousel: the wide shot, then five details that make it work.

14. The client's words.

A quote from the handover, with their permission. One line is enough.

Between projects

15. Your process, in one post.

Intake, design, build, handover. A simple carousel that answers "how do you work?" before anyone asks.

How often to post

Pick a rhythm you can keep. A planned month beats a busy week followed by silence. Batch your photos weekly, plan the month once, and approve posts in one sitting.

A note on AI and editing

AI can fix light, straighten angles and clear site mess. It should never add furniture, change finishes or invent a room. Clients compare your feed with what they receive. Keep those two the same.
